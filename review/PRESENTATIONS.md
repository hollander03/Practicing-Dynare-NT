# Presentation content review (no visual checks)

Scope: all five PDF decks and two PPTX decks in the adjacent 2025 `presentations` folder, including October and 26 November subfolders. Text and speaker notes were extracted. No embedded PDF annotations or PPTX comment parts were found; the participant corrections may be incorporated into the slide content, flattened into images, or held in another version. Image-only marks cannot be assessed without the visual review excluded for this pass. Original decks were not changed.

| Source / page | Finding | Assessment / action |
|---|---|---|
| `oct/RMT_Fiscal_Policy-1.pdf`, p. 2 | Distorting taxes “prevent competitive equilibrium allocation” | Overstatement: taxes distort the allocation relative to an undistorted benchmark, but do not in general prevent a competitive equilibrium. Log for deck correction. |
| Same, p. 3 | `simul` deprecated; missing `shocks` causes errors | Modern setup/solver interface adopted. Do not treat legacy `simul` as necessarily broken: it is an alias. A separate shocks block is not required when initval/endval alone describe the desired permanent transition. |
| Same, p. 3 | Fixed indices “1 for c, 2 for k” | Works only for that declaration order. New files use `strcmp(M_.endo_names,...)` when extracting series. |
| Same, p. 4 eq. 2; `oct/RMT_Fiscal_Policy-2.pdf`, pp. 3–4; `26 Nov/RMT_Fiscal_Policy_labour_extension.pdf`, pp. 3–5 | Dated price printed as beta times next-period consumption marginal utility over current consumption tax | Inconsistent with the worksheet's dated Arrow–Debreu price `beta^t*c_t^(-gamma)/(1+tauc_t)`. Do not use this slide expression as a steady-state recursive price equation. New core solves allocations; yield file constructs normalized dated prices afterward. |
| `RMT_Fiscal_Policy-1.pdf`, p. 4 eq. 5; `RMT_Fiscal_Policy-2.pdf`, p. 3 eq. 5; labour deck p. 3 eq. 5 | Investment instrument signs mix `1+tau` and `1-tau` | Under the stated household investment expenditure `(1-taui)*i`, both investment-price ratios use minus signs. Corrected worksheet equation (11) and section code. |
| `RMT_Fiscal_Policy-1.pdf`, p. 8 eq. 12 | Depreciation term with constant investment subsidy | In steady state, the subsidy ratio in depreciation cancels; rental term divided by `1-taui` is appropriate. Retain under subsidy interpretation. |
| `RMT_Fiscal_Policy-2.pdf`, p. 5; 2025 fiscal `.mod` variants | `R_t` paired with `c(+1)` Euler marginal utility | Wrong horizon for that Euler: the return on today's saving needs next-period taxes and marginal product using today's capital choice. Corrected directly rather than shifting plotted series to conceal kinks. |
| Labour deck p. 6 eq. 20 | Saving return uses next-period capital and labour, while section 4 uses current-date rental | Label and timing must be explicit: distinguish realized current gross return from forward saving return. Not used as an extra recursive state in new code. |
| Labour deck p. 7 | Steady state requires “rolling back n” | Reject as a mathematical steady-state argument: leads and lags have identical values at a constant steady state. Changing timing can alter dynamics but cannot fix an otherwise identical static equation. New log-utility labour examples solve without this workaround. |
| Labour `.mod` revisions | `gamma=2`, occasionally changed `B`, missing consumption-tax wedge; `n(1)` in return | These describe different preferences/calibrations or mistimed returns, not neutral cleanup. Worksheet 5.4 uses log consumption: gamma=1, B=3, and includes consumption-tax wedge. Originals preserved for later comparison. |
| Labour deck pp. 8–12 | Repeated captions and anticipated/unanticipated labels | Worksheet 5.4.1 is unanticipated at text t=0; 5.4.3 anticipated at t=10. Historical plots not certified; new file names distinguish these cases. |
| `RBC.pdf`, p. 3 | `ln G_t = omega ln G_(t-1)+epsilon` with arbitrary nonunit steady G | For log levels, needs `(1-omega)*ln Gbar`; alternatively define g as log deviation, as `basicrbc2015_g_added.mod` does. Log distinction; do not add an intercept to the already linear deviation model. |
| `RBC.pdf`, p. 5 | beta=.994 | Accompanying `basicrbc2015_g_added.mod` sets beta=.99. Log calibration disagreement; no ungrounded choice for replication. |
| `RBC.pdf`, pp. 6–8 | Empirical moments and IRF comparison | Data preparation and source series are not supplied with this deck; no claim of empirical replication. Archived accompanying RBC models as separate exercises. |
| `NKC.pdf`, p. 5 | Natural-rate feedback rule called “optimal simple rule” | No optimisation objective/weight/solved coefficient evidence supplied. Treat as a policy-rule specification pending optimisation evidence. Adjacent MIU file is flexible-price money-growth model, not the claimed six-rule sticky-price NK exercise. Archived as unmatched. |
| `RBC Modelling homework.pptx`; `oct/DSGE TRAINING.pptx` | No embedded comments | Text and speaker notes retained for review. General training/background and homework, not a reliable source of missing Section 2–5 corrections. No worksheet changes inferred from them. |

References: [Dynare deterministic simulation documentation](https://www.dynare.org/manual/the-model-file.html) describes `simul` as a deprecated equivalent to setup plus solver. [MIT Press third edition](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262018746/recursive-macroeconomic-theory/) confirms 2012; worksheet's 20XX and mixed 2018/third-edition citations replaced accordingly. Exact chapter/figure mappings remain for the agreed replication review.

Additional PPTX findings: `RBC Modelling homework.pptx` slide 7 calls the stationary AR(1) technology response permanent and slide 9 describes sustained growth. With persistence below one, a technology innovation is persistent but transitory; it does not permanently raise the model's growth rate. Labour and investment responses also depend on calibration/preferences, so the slide 7–8 directional statements should be presented as results of the specified run rather than general RBC propositions. `oct/DSGE TRAINING.pptx` is a six-slide MIU overview; its variable/policy-function tables and IRF panels are embedded objects/images, so their numerical contents remain unchecked under the no-visual-review constraint.
