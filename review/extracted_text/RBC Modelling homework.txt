BACKGROUND: STANDARD RBC MODELS
RBC tradition:
 Standard RBC models emphasize technology (TFP) shocks as the main driver of business cycles.
Fiscal policy in RBC:
 Government spending is usually passive, financed by lump-sum taxes, with limited role in driving cycles.
Why extend the model?
To examine whether fiscal shocks can generate realistic fluctuations.
To test crowding-out vs. stimulus effects on private activity.

ppt/slides/slide7.xml
IRFs – Technology Shock
A positive productivity shock makes firms more efficient.
Higher productivity raises wages and returns on capital.
Households consume more, invest more, and supply more 
labour
.
Output expands strongly and persists due to the durability of the shock.
Key intuition:
 Technology shocks stimulate demand and supply simultaneously, creating a long-lasting boost in output (permanent output shock deviation from the baseline remain above 0 percentage points over the long-run).

ppt/slides/slide8.xml
IRFs – Government Spending Shock
Higher government spending directly raises demand.
Households supply more 
labour
 due to the wealth effect.
Wages fall while employment increases.
Private consumption and investment are crowded out.
Output rises temporarily, but the effect fades as the spending shock is not permanent.
Key intuition: 
Government expenditure shocks boost output in the short run by lowering real wages and raising 
labour
 supply, but do not create lasting growth.

Comparison and Conclusion
In summary
Tech shocks → sustained growth, private activity boosted
Govt shocks → short-term stimulus, private consumption crowded out.
